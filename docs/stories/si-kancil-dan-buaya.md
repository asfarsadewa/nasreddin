# Si Kancil dan Buaya — production note

## Source note

Si Kancil belongs to the wider Southeast Asian mousedeer trickster cycle rather than to one historical period, court, or named locality. This production follows the compact Indonesian classroom telling, “The Fable of the Mousedeer and the Crocodiles,” published in the British Council’s 2019 *Indonesia Education Pack* with Indonesia’s National Book Committee. In that version, the mousedeer is travelling to visit his grandfather; crocodiles close in to eat him; he turns their dispute over equal portions into a counting problem; they line up across the river; and he counts them by crossing on their backs. The pack’s contributor credit names Faza for the fable.

Other familiar Indonesian and Malay tellings send Kancil across the river for food, have him claim that a king is preparing a feast or taking a census, or end with a speech revealing the trick. Those elements are omitted. The eight crocodiles and their difficulty keeping numbers in order come from the selected 2019 telling. No island, kingdom, costume, architecture, or concluding moral is invented. “Kancil” is retained in every title and performance; the procedural animal is shaped as a small mousedeer rather than a European deer.

The contradiction is spatial and audible: the predators believe that arranging themselves will make one small body easier to divide, while their own ordered backs turn the dangerous river into the only bridge Kancil needs. The count is both the trick and the film’s movement score.

Source: [British Council, *Indonesia Education Pack*, pp. 16–18](https://www.britishcouncil.org/sites/default/files/indonesia_educational_pack_19-i5.pdf). Historical context for the wider literary mousedeer cycle is noted by the public-domain 1893 edition of [*Hikayat Pelanduk Jenaka*](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hikayat_pelanduk_jenaka.pdf), but this film does not claim that the selected classroom episode is a transcription of that manuscript.

## Visual grammar

- Palette: river ink `#081c24`, mangrove green `#173f36`, wet jade `#2d6658`, kancil russet `#ad5d36`, dusk brass `#e5b95f`, reed straw `#c8b77d`, crocodile moss `#526640`.
- Materials: wet low-poly river stone, lacquered leaves, fibrous reeds, matte animal silhouettes, and restrained brass light; no raster imagery.
- Composition: every early shot makes the banks feel disconnected. The crocodiles first form a loose enclosing arc, then snap into the strong central line that connects both banks. Kancil’s motion changes from trapped lateral glances to decisive forward leaps.
- Signature transition: eight dynamic crocodile rigs assemble into a living bridge. Every counted landing emits instanced concentric ripples that remain briefly in the river, turning the spoken numbers into a visible path.
- Instancing: the crocodile chorus shares bodies, heads, jaws, tails, eyes, and scutes through dynamic `THREE.InstancedMesh` batches. Forest trunks, canopy clusters, reeds, stones, lotus leaves, fireflies, and count ripples are also instanced. Si Kancil and the lead crocodile remain authored, expressive rigs.
- Camera grammar: each beat has an authored entry and exit pose, target, and field of view. Smooth per-shot dollies and cranes replace one continuous sightseeing orbit; the crossing alternates river sides to make each counted half spatially legible.
- Default playback: Indonesian voice with English subtitles.
